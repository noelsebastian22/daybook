Daybook is a to-do app I build and run myself. I'm Noel Sebastian, and I'm in the Blue Mountains, NSW, Australia. This page covers what Daybook keeps about you, where it goes, and how to have it removed.

The short version: it keeps what it needs to show you your days. Nothing is sold, and there are no ads, analytics or trackers.

What Daybook stores

  • Your account. Your email address. If you sign in with Google, also the name and profile picture link Google shares when you sign in.
  • Your tasks. What you write, any notes, the day each one is on, when you finished it, its category, energy tag and reminder time, and how many times it has carried over or been pushed.
  • Your history. A record of each day once it closes: what you finished and what carried over. The calendar and reports are built from it.
  • Your settings. Your time zone, your categories, and whether and when to send you the morning email.
  • Reminder devices. If you turn on reminders, the push address your browser gives Daybook, one for each device.
  • Access requests. If you ask to be let in, your email, the note you write, and the IP address the request came from. The IP is only used to stop the same person sending request after request.
  • Connected apps. If you connect Claude or another app to Daybook, a record of that connection and what you approved.

What it doesn't do

  • No analytics, advertising or tracking scripts. The fonts are served by Daybook itself.
  • Your data is never sold, rented or shared for marketing.
  • I don't browse your tasks. Running the service means I can reach the database, and I'll only look at your data to fix a problem you've asked me about, or if the law requires it.

Who else handles it

Daybook runs on a few services, each doing one job:

  • Supabase holds the database and handles sign-in. Your data is stored in its Sydney region.
  • Vercel serves the app. Like any web host, it sees your IP address and browser in its request logs.
  • Resend sends the morning email and the access emails, so it handles your email address and what's in those emails, including task text.
  • Google, only if you sign in with Google.
  • Your browser's push service (Apple, Google or Mozilla, depending on the browser) delivers reminders. Each reminder is encrypted before it leaves Daybook, so the push service can't read it.
  • Anthropic, only if you connect Claude yourself. What Claude reads through that connection is then covered by Anthropic's own privacy policy.

Some of these services process data outside Australia.

On your device

Daybook keeps your sign-in session, your theme and sidebar choice, and any changes you made while offline in your browser's local storage. Nothing there is used to track you, and signing out clears the session.

Keeping it, and deleting it

Your data stays for as long as you have an account. The morning email and reminders can be turned off at any time in Settings.

To delete your account, email me from the address you sign in with. I'll delete the account and everything in it (tasks, history, settings and devices) and let you know when it's done. Copies can linger in the providers' routine backups for a short time before those expire.

You can also ask what Daybook holds about you, ask for a copy of it, or ask me to correct something. Same address.
